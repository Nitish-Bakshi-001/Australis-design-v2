Auction day: what to sort before you raise your hand

RV Ravinder Pal Kaur Virk 30 April 2026 6 min read

Buying at auction is different in one crucial way: there's no cooling-off. The moment the hammer falls, you're bound. That's why everything has to be sorted before you raise your hand.

An auction is exciting, fast and final. Win, and you'll typically sign the contract and pay the deposit on the spot — with no opportunity to change your mind afterward. The buyers who bid with confidence are the ones who did the work in advance.

Get the contract reviewed first

Because there's no safety net, the contract and Section 32 must be checked before auction day, not after. We read them for anything that could affect value or carry risk — easements, special conditions, owners-corporation issues — so you know exactly what you're bidding on.

Have your finance locked down

There's no "subject to finance" at auction. You need formal approval in place and certainty about your limit before you bid. We'll also confirm the deposit amount and how it must be paid on the day.

Your auction-day checklist

  • Contract and Section 32 reviewed and any concerns resolved;
  • Formal finance approval confirmed — not just pre-approval;
  • Deposit funds ready, in the form the contract requires;
  • Any requested contract changes negotiated with the agent beforehand;
  • A clear maximum price you won't go past.
“At auction, preparation is your only protection. By the time you're bidding, every question should already be answered.”

If you win

You'll sign immediately and pay the deposit, and the contract is unconditional from that moment. From there we move straight into settlement — confirming dates, coordinating with your lender, and steering everything through to the day you collect the keys.
