Costs & Stamp Duty

What the 2026 Victorian duty
changes mean for off-the-plan
buyers.

RV Ravinder Pal Kaur Virk 10 June 2026 6 min read

The headline numbers in this year's duty changes are easy to find — but they're only half the story. What actually moves the needle for an off-the-plan buyer is the deposit you pay, the concessions you can claim, and the timing of your contract. Here's how the three fit together.

Off-the-plan purchases work differently from buying an established home, and Victorian land transfer duty is where that difference shows up most. Because you're buying before construction is finished, the duty can be calculated on a lower base — which, for the right buyer, can mean a meaningfully smaller bill at settlement.

How off-the-plan duty is calculated

For eligible buyers, duty on an off-the-plan purchase is assessed on the contract price less the construction or refurbishment costs that haven't yet been incurred when you sign. The earlier in the build you commit, the larger that deduction tends to be — and the lower the dutiable value.

The concession only applies where you intend to live in the property as your principal place of residence (or, in some cases, where you qualify as a first home buyer). Investors generally don't access the same deduction, so the structure of your purchase matters as much as the price.

“The single most expensive mistake we see is a buyer assuming the off-the-plan concession applies — and budgeting for a duty figure they were never going to get.”

The three things that actually change your bill

When we review an off-the-plan contract, three factors decide what you'll really pay:

  • When you sign. The further the build is from completion, the higher the undeducted construction cost — and the lower your dutiable value.
  • How you'll use it. Principal-place-of-residence and first-home-buyer status unlock concessions an investor purchase can't.
  • What the contract says. The way construction costs are disclosed and apportioned in the contract directly affects the State Revenue Office assessment.

Timing and your deposit

Off-the-plan settlements can be months — sometimes years — away. That gap affects more than duty: your deposit is typically held until settlement, finance approvals can lapse and need renewing, and the market may move in either direction before you get the keys. The bottom line

The 2026 settings don't change the fundamentals: off-the-plan can be a genuinely tax-effective way to buy, but only when the concession actually applies and the contract is structured to support it. Get those right and the saving is real. Get them wrong and you're budgeting for a discount you'll never receive.
