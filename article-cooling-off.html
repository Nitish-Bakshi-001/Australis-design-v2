Cooling-off periods in Victoria, explained

RV Ravinder Pal Kaur Virk 28 May 2026 4 min read

The cooling-off period is one of the most misunderstood parts of buying a home in Victoria. Here's when the clock starts, when it doesn't apply at all, and what walking away actually costs.

For most private-sale residential purchases in Victoria, you get a short cooling-off period after signing the contract. It's a safety valve — a few days to change your mind. But it comes with real conditions, and several common situations switch it off entirely.

When the clock starts

The cooling-off period generally runs for three clear business days from the day you sign the contract — not the day the seller signs, and not the day you paid your deposit. Getting that start date right matters, because the window is short.

When it doesn't apply

There are important exceptions. Cooling-off usually does not apply when you:

  • Buy at a public auction, or within three clear business days before or after one;
  • Buy a property used mainly for industrial or commercial purposes;
  • Buy certain rural land above a set size;
  • Have already signed a contract for the same property under similar terms.

What it costs to walk away

If you do cool off within the window, you can end the contract — but the seller is entitled to keep a small penalty (a set percentage of the purchase price or a fixed sum, whichever is more). The rest of your deposit is refunded. It's rarely free, but it's far cheaper than being locked into the wrong purchase.

“At auction there is no cooling-off. That's exactly why the contract work has to be done before you raise your hand — not after.”
