First home buyer grants & concessions: a quick map

RV Ravinder Pal Kaur Virk 14 May 2026 7 min read

First home buyer support in Victoria comes in a few different forms — and they don't all work the same way. Here's a plain-English map of what's out there, who qualifies, and how the pieces fit together.

The headline schemes change from time to time, and eligibility always comes down to the detail of your purchase. But the shape of the support has been consistent: a grant for new homes, a duty concession scaled to price, and a couple of programs that help with the deposit.

The grant for new builds

The First Home Owner Grant is a one-off payment for eligible buyers purchasing or building a brand-new home up to a set value. It doesn't apply to established homes, so it's most relevant if you're buying off-the-plan or building.

The stamp-duty concession

This is where most first home buyers save the most. Eligible buyers may pay no duty on a home up to a threshold value, with a concession that tapers on a sliding scale above it. The key factors are the purchase price, whether you'll live there, and your residency status.

Deposit-support programs

Separate from grants and concessions, there are programs designed to help you buy with a smaller deposit — some let eligible buyers purchase without paying lenders mortgage insurance. These have their own caps and limited places, so timing matters.

How they stack

The important thing is that these supports can often be combined — but not always, and the interaction depends on what and how you buy:

  • The grant generally applies only to new homes; the duty concession can apply to new or established;
  • Price thresholds differ between schemes, so a property can qualify for one and not another;
  • You typically must move in within a set period and live there for a minimum time;
  • Eligibility is assessed per person, so a co-buyer's history can affect the outcome.
