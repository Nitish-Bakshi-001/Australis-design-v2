FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick, honest answers to the questions we hear most about buying, selling and transferring property in Victoria.

Ask your own question
Ideally before you sign a contract or bid at auction. We'll review the contract and Section 32 first so you know exactly what you're committing to — and your first review is free.
A Section 32 (vendor's statement) is a document the seller must give you before sale. It discloses key facts about the property — title details, rates, easements, owners-corporation costs and any notices affecting the land. We read it line by line and translate the important parts.
For most private-sale residential purchases in Victoria you get a short cooling-off period after signing, during which you can withdraw (a small penalty may apply). It generally doesn't apply at auction or within three clear business days of one. We'll confirm exactly how it applies to you.
Before your property can be advertised for sale, a Section 32 vendor's statement must be prepared. We'll gather the title, council and owners-corporation information and prepare a compliant statement so you're ready to go to market without delay.
Land transfer duty in Victoria is calculated on a sliding scale based on the property's value, with concessions for first home buyers, principal places of residence and certain off-the-plan purchases. We'll estimate your duty up front and check every concession you may be eligible for.
Eligible first home buyers may pay no duty on homes up to a set value, with a concession on a sliding scale above that. Eligibility depends on the purchase price, residency and other criteria — we'll confirm what applies to your purchase.
Disbursements are the third-party costs of your transaction — title searches, council and water certificates, and government lodgement fees. We pass these on at cost, itemised clearly, and never add a markup.
A licensed conveyancer specialises specifically in property transfers — it's all we do. For standard residential and commercial transactions, that focus usually means a sharper, more cost-effective service. For complex legal disputes a solicitor may be needed, and we'll tell you honestly if that's the case.
Most Victorian settlements run on a 30–90 day timeline set in the contract. Off-the-plan purchases can take considerably longer. We'll map out your exact dates from day one.
