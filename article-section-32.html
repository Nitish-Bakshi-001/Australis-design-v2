Section 32: the five things we always check first

RV Ravinder Pal Kaur Virk 3 June 2026 5 min read

A Section 32 — the vendor's statement — is the most important document you'll read before buying in Victoria. It's also where the expensive surprises hide. These are the five things we read before anything else.

By law, a seller must give you a Section 32 statement before you sign. It discloses what the property comes with — and what it comes attached to. Most buyers skim it; we read every line, because a single clause can change what you're really buying.

1. Title and ownership

We confirm the seller actually owns what they're selling, and check the title for anything registered against it — mortgages, caveats, covenants or owners-corporation interests. A restrictive covenant, for example, can quietly limit what you're allowed to build.

2. Easements and encumbrances

An easement gives someone else a right over the land — often a drain, a right of way, or services running underneath. Build over the wrong one and you may be forced to remove it at your own cost. We map exactly where they sit.

3. Planning and zoning

The zone and any overlays tell you what the property can be used for and what restrictions apply. An overlay can affect everything from renovations to whether a tree can be removed — important if you're buying with plans in mind.

4. Rates, outgoings and owners corporation

We check the running costs: council and water rates, land tax, and — for units and apartments — owners-corporation fees and any special levies. A looming special levy for building works is exactly the kind of thing that should shape your offer.

5. Notices affecting the property

Finally, we look for any notices or orders — from council, an authority or a building surveyor — that affect the land. An outstanding notice can become your problem the moment you settle.

“The contract tells you the price. The Section 32 tells you what you're actually buying. Read the second one first.”
